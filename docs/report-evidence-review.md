# Supplied report evidence review

All five supplied PDFs were extracted, then every page was visually inspected through contact sheets. The review records what each method can establish and prevents historical scenarios from entering the current ledger as observations.

## IRENA — Renewable Energy Outlook: Lebanon (2020)

This country-led Renewable Readiness Assessment combines stakeholder consultation with a bottom-up REmap analysis using 2014 as its base year. Its prominent 2030 case includes 2,500 MW central solar PV, 500 MW decentralised PV, 1,000 MW wind, 601 MW hydro and 13 MW biogas, with average annual power-sector savings of USD 249 million and roughly USD 2.2 billion of additional investment.

Use it for the historic barrier assessment, technology potential and a comparable planning scenario. Do not treat it as a current forecast. Grid-upgrade costs are excluded, dispatch and unit commitment are not modeled, decentralised solar is assigned zero cost in the system comparison, agriculture is omitted for lack of data, and several assumptions are explicitly approximate or dated.

## AUB IFI — Pathways for Increasing the Utilization of Distributed Renewable Energy in Lebanon (June 2026)

This is the most current supplied document, but it contains several distinct evidence designs: a three-house logger sample, a 1,000-household survey, a village bi-level model and a hybrid-system case study. The household survey reports at least 41% experiencing curtailment and unmet demand that could reach 50%. The village model explores a 90% curtailment reduction, a 10% tariff reduction and an 8–14 US¢/kWh feed-in tariff.

Use it for current distributed-energy constraints and policy questions. Keep each result attached to its method. The three-home finding cannot be nationalised, survey responses are not meter totals, and modeled tariffs are not official rules. The document also records conflicting demand estimates and uses some unofficial EDL information, which the Atlas preserves as uncertainty.

## SDG7 Energy Compact of the Republic of Lebanon (2021)

The compact records voluntary commitments, including renewables equal to 30% of power demand and 16.5% of building heat by 2030, a 10% power-demand reduction and conditional deployment of 500 MW of rooftop generation to replace diesel generators. It also describes enabling legislation, an ERA, certification and finance.

Use it to trace stated ambition in 2021. It is neither legislation nor evidence that the targets were financed, implemented or achieved. Its USD 2.2 billion cost indication excludes grid investment.

## UNDP — Derisking Renewable Energy Investment: Lebanon (2017)

The study combines structured investor/developer interviews with a quantitative risk-informed finance model for 450 MW wind and 300 MW solar. Under its historical assumptions, public measures reduce modeled wind financing costs from 11.4 to 9.4 US¢/kWh and solar from 10.0 to 8.2 US¢/kWh. It estimates USD 98 million and USD 46 million of public costs against USD 221 million and USD 97 million of savings respectively.

Use it for the original risk taxonomy and historical counterfactual. Its 2015–2016 risk and financing conditions are not current, and the authors explicitly describe it as one input to policy rather than a definitive analysis.

## FES — Sustainable Transformation of Lebanon’s Energy System (2021)

The study combines literature and data review, structured expert interviews and a four-phase transition model: renewable-energy take-off, system integration, power-to-fuels/gases and movement toward 100% renewables. It classified Lebanon in the early stage of the first phase at publication.

Use it as a conceptual and qualitative diagnosis. A transition phase is not a legal status, implementation score or quantitative forecast.

## Editorial consequence

The public product classifies the five reports as official scenario, policy brief, voluntary commitment, modeled analysis and conceptual study. Numerical extracts enter the claim or metric ledger only when their method, period, precision and caveat are displayed with them.
